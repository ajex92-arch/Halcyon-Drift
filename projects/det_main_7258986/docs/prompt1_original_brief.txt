You are building a Godot 4.x prototype using GDScript.

**Game concept:** Top-down 2D large-scale space combat hybrid of Homeworld 2 (map scale, ship sizes, speeds, fleet feel) + Natural Selection 2 (commander + resource capture) + Counter-Strike (individual loadout purchasing).

**Core fantasy:** You are one pilot in a 32v32 battle. There is also one Admiral per team who controls the mothership and spends the team treasury. Pilots have meaningful independent options so the game does not fully collapse if the Admiral is weak.

**Prototype scope (important):** 
Do not attempt real 64-player networking yet. Build a local prototype where the player controls one ship and the rest of both teams are simple AI. Focus on making the systems feel good with ~8–16 ships on the map at once.

**Camera & Scale:**
- Top-down camera with zoom (mouse wheel) and pan. Scale similar to Homeworld 2: ships should feel small relative to the map; travel times between points should take 20–60+ seconds depending on ship type.
- Smooth camera follow with optional edge panning. Default zoom should keep the game readable and playable without constant zooming.

**Flight Model (easy to learn, hard to master):**
Design the movement so a new player can fly to a node and fight within the first minute, while experienced players gain clear advantages through momentum management, positioning, and ship-specific handling.

- **Controls:** Mouse aims ship facing (or a reticle). W accelerates forward in the facing direction. S applies reverse thrust or braking. A/D (or Q/E) activate lateral thrusters/strafe. Optional boost key if the ship has one.
- **Physics:** Newtonian-lite. Ships have noticeable momentum — stopping or changing direction at speed takes time and space. Acceleration, max speed, and turn rate differ strongly by ship class. Soft speed cap with brief ability to exceed it via boost or thrust vectoring. Lateral thrusters are weaker than main engines on most ships.
- **Skill expression:** Facing and velocity can be partially decoupled. Players can drift, kite, or approach at controlled speeds. Good pilots manage arrival angle and speed at nodes/fights so they don’t overshoot capture radii or repair bays. Ship classes reward different styles (Interceptor: constant micro and strafing; Bomber: planned attack runs; Heavy: commitment and anchoring).
- **Readability aids:** Strong visual velocity indicator (arrow or line showing current direction and relative speed). Clear thruster visuals when main or lateral engines fire. Light optional flight assist (reduces unwanted rotation or helps cancel lateral velocity) that can be toggled or is weaker on agile ships.
- **Forgiveness:** Light bumping off asteroids and other ships rather than hard stops. Basic forward-thrust flying must still be viable for participating in nodes and combat; advanced movement is an advantage, not a requirement.
- **Implementation note:** Prioritize getting acceleration, momentum, turn rates, and the velocity indicator feeling good before adding deeper systems. Use CharacterBody2D or kinematic movement with custom velocity handling.

**Map Design Constraints:**
- Large open map with Homeworld-like scale. Crossing the full map should take a meaningful amount of time even for fast ships (roughly 45–90 seconds for interceptors, longer for heavier ships).
- Two motherships placed on opposite sides of the map with clear, relatively safe approach corridors but no completely sealed spawn protection.
- 5–7 resource nodes (asteroid clusters) distributed across the map. At least 2 should be relatively close to each mothership (easier to defend early), with the rest in contested mid-map or flanking positions.
- Nodes should not be perfectly symmetric if it reduces interesting decision-making, but neither side should have a clearly superior set of starting nodes.
- Include areas of relative cover (dense asteroid fields, debris clusters, or nebula-like regions that reduce sensor range or block line of sight for certain weapons). These should create tactical positioning without turning the map into a maze.
- Leave large stretches of open space so capital-ship-scale weapons and maneuvering still matter.
- Support multiple routes between major nodes so teams can choose to contest directly, flank, or split forces.
- Place at least one high-value or high-risk “central” or offset node that is worth fighting over but dangerous to hold.
- Design the map so that 8–16 ships on screen still feel like they are operating across a large theater rather than a small arena. Avoid packing all objectives into a tight central area.
- Include space for 1–2 dynamic events (moving debris, temporary sensor-blackout zones, or a spawnable derelict) without overcrowding the static layout.
- Ensure clear visual distinction between friendly/enemy controlled nodes, open space, and cover areas even with placeholder art.

**Core Loop:**
1. Players spawn on their team’s mothership.
2. They spend personal money on the mothership to buy a ship and modules (CS-style buy menu).
3. They fly out, fight, contest asteroid resource nodes, and can perform limited independent logistics actions.
4. Admiral spends team treasury on mothership upgrades, ship unlocks, team-wide buffs, and permanent forward structures.
5. Income comes from resource nodes, kill/assist rewards, and objective contribution.

**Resource Nodes (asteroid belts):**
- Several small asteroid clusters act as capturable resource points.
- Capturing requires staying in a radius for a period while uncontested. Contested nodes pause capture.
- Once captured, the Admiral can spend treasury to build permanent structures:
  - Resource extractor (team income)
  - Automated extractor (higher income, no players required nearby)
  - Repair bay (players can repair and swap modules here)
  - Basic defenses (turrets)
- Nodes also contain a small number of “hardpoints” or asteroid chunks. Individual ships can mine these for personal money (slower and riskier than Admiral extractors). Destroying enemy structures on a node grants a significant personal bounty and a temporary team income spike.
- Income split from extractors: 60% to team treasury, 40% split equally among living players on the team as personal income over time.
- Players also earn personal money from kills, assists (percentage of damage dealt), and objective contribution (holding or defending nodes, protecting extractors).

**Pilot Independent Logistics (limited):**
Pilots can spend personal money on temporary, weaker versions of team tools:
- Short-duration personal repair kits or deployable repair drones
- Small, fragile personal extractors that generate money while alive but die quickly
- One-time drops that mark a node and give a small temporary buff to allies defending it
These exist so pilots can create emergency value without waiting on the Admiral. The Admiral’s permanent structures remain clearly superior.

**Admiral Role (one per team):**
- Special UI for the Admiral.
- Can spend treasury on:
  - Ship type unlocks
  - Upgrades for specific ship classes
  - Team upgrades (global speed, sensor range, starting money, etc.)
  - Permanent extractors, defenses, and repair bays at captured nodes
- Can place temporary waypoints. Ships that stay near an Admiral waypoint receive a small visible benefit (slight speed, repair rate, or sensor range). This rewards following orders without full RTS control.
- Admiral can still fly a ship if desired, but their main power is the command UI and treasury spending.

**Ship System & Roles:**
Start with a small set of clearly differentiated roles (unlock more via Admiral):

- **Interceptor**: Fast, fragile, strong vs. small ships. High acceleration, tight turns, strong strafe. Good for spotting and quickly contesting nodes.
- **Bomber / Strike**: Slower turning, strong forward thrust, high damage vs. structures and larger ships. Rewards planned attack runs. Benefits from Admiral sensor support or marked targets.
- **Logistics**: Weak combat, moderate handling, strong at repairing allies and deploying temporary structures. Amplifies Admiral builds.
- **Support / EW**: Sensor range, jamming, or small area buffs. Handling supports staying at useful ranges. Makes the Admiral’s vision and orders more effective.
- **Heavy / Frigate**: High momentum, durable, weaker strafe, area damage or anti-fighter capability. Strong at holding captured nodes through commitment and positioning.

Each ship has health, speed, acceleration, turn rate, weapon type, damage, and optional special ability. Players buy ships + modules with personal money. Limited mid-match module swapping is allowed at mothership or Admiral-built repair bays. Handling differences between classes should be obvious within 10–15 seconds of flying each one.

**Mothership & Pacing:**
- Mothership has strong fixed defenses but becomes temporarily weaker after large treasury spends or after launching many ships.
- Damaging the enemy mothership enough can force a temporary “retreat” phase where the enemy loses full access to it and must rely on forward bases.
- Destroying the enemy mothership ends the match (or use score-based alternative).

**Additional Systems:**
- Shared vision from Support ships and Admiral.
- One or two simple dynamic map events (moving debris field, temporary sensor blackout, or a high-value derelict) to vary node importance.
- Temporary personal “ace” or streak bonuses that are ship-based and fade over time (keep individual skill expression high without permanent power spikes).

**Win Conditions (prototype options):**
- Destroy the enemy mothership, or
- Highest score after X minutes (nodes held + kills + structures destroyed), or
- Hold majority of nodes for a sustained period.

**Technical priorities for the first version:**
1. Flight model that feels good (momentum, class differences, velocity indicator, readable controls) + camera that supports Homeworld scale
2. Map layout that supports the scale, node distribution, and cover rules above
3. Mothership buy menu (personal money) + basic ship roles with distinct handling
4. Simple AI ships that fly toward nodes or enemies using the same movement rules
5. Capturable resource nodes with hardpoints, mining, and basic income
6. Pilot temporary logistics options
7. Admiral UI that can spend treasury, build structures, and place waypoints with incentives
8. Basic combat (shooting, damage, death, respawn, assists)

**Visual style:** Placeholder shapes and colors are fine. Different ship silhouettes, clear thruster particles for main and lateral engines, velocity indicator, simple projectiles, clear node ownership colors, and visible waypoint benefits. Prioritize readability of movement and positioning over art.

**Constraints:**
- GDScript only
- Godot 4.x
- Keep systems modular (separate scenes for ships, nodes, UI, structures, map elements, etc.)
- Comment major systems so they can be extended later
